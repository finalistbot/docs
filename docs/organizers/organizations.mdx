# Organizations

Scrims belong to an **organization**, never to a person. Before you can host anything you
need one, and if you're on a team with other admins, the org is what you share.

## Create one

There are two ways to get an organization, and they differ in where you start.

### On the web

On <a href={links.manage}>app.finalist.live</a>, create an organization with a name and a
**slug**. The slug is lowercase and unique, and it becomes your public address:

```
https://finalist.live/o/your-slug
```

![Creating an organization](./assets/create-org.png)

You are the **owner** of any org you create this way.

### From Discord

Invite the bot to a Discord server, then **claim** it — with `/claim` in Discord, or from
the web with the Discord account that added the bot. Claiming is what creates the
organization: named after the server, with a slug derived from that name, owned by whoever
claimed it.

Inviting the bot on its own creates nothing. The install is recorded and the server sits
**pending** until somebody claims it, which is why a drive-by install never leaves you
owning an org you didn't ask for.

This is the shortest path from nothing to hosting, and it's covered in
[Connect your server](../discord/connect-server).

### Your public page

Either way, the org gets a public page showing your identity, the scrims you're hosting, and
your player and team leaderboards. Anyone can view it without an account.

![The public organization page](./assets/org-public-page.png)

## Switching organizations

You can belong to several organizations. The management app always acts on **one active
organization** at a time, picked from the switcher. Everything you do in `/manage` applies
to whichever org is active, so glance at the switcher before you create a scrim.

## Members and roles

Members join by **invitation only**. There's no request-to-join flow, and no public sign-up.

| Role | Can do |
|------|--------|
| **Owner** | Everything, including handing the org to someone else. |
| **Admin** | Every org action except transferring ownership. |
| **Moderator** | A limited set of moderation actions. |

There is exactly one owner at a time, and an organization always has one — the account that
created it, or the account that claimed the Discord server it came from.

![Organization members and their roles](./assets/org-members.png)

### Transferring ownership

Only the current owner can transfer, and only to somebody who is **already a member**. Invite
them first if they aren't.

The swap happens in one move: the person you name becomes the owner, and you stay in the org
as an **admin**. You both get told. Like every other consequential action, it lands in the
audit log.

### Inviting

Invite by username, and search picks the account. Choose the role when you send it. Invites
**expire after 7 days**, and you can cancel a pending one at any time.

![Inviting a member](./assets/org-invite.png)

The invited person finds it under **Inbox** on the dashboard, where it sits with a pending
badge until they accept or decline. Opening the Inbox clears the sidebar's unread count, and
a new invite raises it again. You're notified either way.

## Branding

Give the organization a name, logo, banner and timezone. The timezone matters more than the
rest: it's what recurring scrim schedules are computed against.

## Bans

Organizations keep their own ban lists, separate from Discord's.

- **Team bans** stop a banned team registering for any of your scrims.
- **Player bans** stop a banned player registering, and a team carrying one is rejected.

![The player ban list, with team bans on the neighbouring tab](./assets/org-bans.png)

Bans are per-organization. Banning someone from your org does not affect any other org.

## Reports and moderation

Players report each other from inside your scrims, and those reports land in **your** queue
— there's no platform tier above you. See [Player reports](./reports) and
[Moderating scrim chat](../scrims/chat-moderation).

## Audit log

Every consequential action is recorded: org created, claimed or updated, ownership transferred,
member roles changed, members removed, invites sent, accepted, declined or cancelled, and every
ban and unban. Each entry records who did it and when.

There's also an access log showing member activity in the org.

## Connecting Discord

An org can connect Discord servers to receive announcements, choose which channels get what,
ping roles, lock channels when registration closes, and give the bot a per-server nickname
and avatar. See [Connect your server](../discord/connect-server).
